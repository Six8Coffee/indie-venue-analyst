# Systems Landscape — What Independent Operators Actually Use

A map of the tools independent venue operators use across markets, and where the gaps are. Use this to understand the existing ecosystem before assessing whether a new product has space.

---

## Point of Sale (POS)

**Common tools:** Square, Lightspeed, Toast (US), Revel, Kounta/Lightspeed (AU), Epos Now (UK), Clover

**What operators actually use it for:** Taking payments. Occasionally running end-of-day reports.

**What they almost never use:** Table management, inventory tracking, integrated loyalty, advanced reporting, API integrations.

**Gap:** POS data is rich and almost entirely wasted. Operators don't have the time or skill to extract insight from it, and most POS providers make reporting hard to access without an expensive tier upgrade.

---

## Accounting

**Common tools:** Xero (AU/UK dominant), MYOB (AU), QuickBooks (US/UK), Wave (budget US)

**What operators actually use it for:** Payroll, BAS/GST lodgement, bank reconciliation. Usually managed by a bookkeeper or accountant.

**What they almost never use:** Budgeting, cash flow forecasting, job costing, P&L analysis.

**Gap:** Accounting software is used reactively, not proactively. Operators see their numbers after the fact, not in time to act on them.

---

## Rostering & Labour

**Common tools:** Deputy (AU/UK), Tanda (AU), 7shifts (US/CA), Planday (EU/UK), Homebase (US small end), spreadsheets

**Adoption pattern:** Operators with 15+ staff are more likely to have a rostering tool. Under 10 staff, WhatsApp and a shared Google Sheet are the most common "system."

**Gap:** The tools that exist are well-built for mid-size venues but over-engineered for small operators. There is no tool that handles the callout-and-scramble workflow elegantly. Communication is still the unsolved layer.

---

## Reservations & Table Management

**Common tools:** OpenTable, Resy (US), SevenRooms, ResDiary (AU/UK), Nowait/Yelp Waitlist, direct phone/email

**What operators actually use it for:** Taking bookings, managing the waitlist on busy nights.

**Gap:** Reservation data (covers, no-show rate, party size, time of day) is almost never analysed. It sits in the tool and gets forgotten.

---

## Supplier Ordering

**Common tools:** MarketMan, Ordermentum (AU), Cut+Dry (US), paper order forms, phone calls, WhatsApp to supplier reps

**Adoption:** Very low. Most independent operators still call or message suppliers directly. Structured ordering tools are underadopted because the switching cost feels high and the supplier reps resist it.

**Gap:** Spend tracking, price comparison, and delivery reconciliation are almost entirely manual. This is one of the most underserved categories in the stack.

---

## HR & Onboarding

**Common tools:** Employment Hero (AU), BambooHR (US, mid-market), paper onboarding packs, WhatsApp induction, nothing structured

**Gap:** Staff onboarding at independent venues is almost entirely informal. New hire documentation, policy acknowledgement, and compliance training happen ad hoc or not at all. High staff turnover makes this an ongoing cost that operators don't quantify.

---

## Communications

**Common tools:** WhatsApp (dominant for internal team comms globally), Instagram DMs (customer inquiries), email, Slack (rare, larger venues only)

**Gap:** WhatsApp is used for everything — rosters, callouts, supplier conversations, shift swaps, feedback. It works until it doesn't. There is no structure, no audit trail, and no separation of signal from noise.

---

## Food Safety & Compliance

**Common tools:** Paper logs, Excel/Sheets templates, Vault (AU), FoodDocs, SafetyCulture/iAuditor (mid-market)

**Adoption:** Paper-first for most small operators. Digital compliance tools are used by operators who have been audited and failed, or who have a more systems-minded owner.

**Gap:** Daily logs are often completed retrospectively. Certification tracking is informal. Regulatory differences between markets make this a hard category to build across borders.

---

## Market-Specific Notes

| Market | Notable differences |
|---|---|
| Australia | Award rate complexity (Modern Awards, penalty rates) creates significant payroll compliance burden; Xero dominant for accounting; Ordermentum gaining traction for supplier ordering |
| United Kingdom | Zero-hours contracts common; holiday pay calculation is a compliance minefield; Planday and Deputy both strong; VAT adds accounting complexity |
| United States | Tips and tip pooling create payroll complexity; state-by-state labour law variation; Toast POS is dominant in restaurants; tipping culture affects customer-facing tech |
| Europe (ex-UK) | GDPR adds data handling requirements; strong union culture in some markets affects rostering; language fragmentation makes pan-European tools hard to build |
